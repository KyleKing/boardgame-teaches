# Risk: Europe — Teach Card

2–4 players · Win by controlling **7+ crowns at the end of a round**

______________________________________________________________________

## Setup (5 minutes)

Each player takes an army, war banner, reminder card, and **8 King's Orders
cards**, plus 5 silver, 1 crown, 1 castle.

**Bid for first player** — secretly hold out coins from your five, reveal
simultaneously.
Highest bidder pays their bid and takes the First Player Marker; everyone else
keeps their coins.
Ties roll off.

Then, in order, each player places their kingdom completely before the next:
pick a **Gold Crown City**, put your crown and castle there, take its City Bonus
Tile and its Tax Value in coins, war banner on space 1 of the Crown Tracker
(**space 2 if you took Rome**), and split 10 footmen between the city and one
adjacent non-city territory.

______________________________________________________________________

## The loop — this is what makes it not Risk

Each round, everyone secretly stacks **two order cards**, face down.
Top card resolves on Turn 1, bottom on Turn 2.
**You cannot reorder them.** Then:

1. **Turn 1** — first player flips their top card, picks *one* of its two orders,
   announces it, resolves it, plus any bonus action.
   Clockwise.
1. **Turn 2** — same with the bottom card.
1. **All battles resolve.** First player chooses the order battles are fought in.
1. Discard both cards.
   You start next round with two fewer options.

When your hand runs out, take all eight back.
That happens every fifth round.

**The consequence to say out loud:** the pairing *is* your plan for the round,
and the order inside the pairing decides whether you can afford it.
Tax must go on top if you intend to Spend.

______________________________________________________________________

## The order-pairing playbook

| Pairing | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Tax → Spend** | The economy round. Your default round 1 |
| **Spend → Expand** | Buy the stack, then push it out immediately |
| **Expand → Expand** | Double land grab. Fast, but you earn nothing and fight next round broke |
| **Expand → Manoeuvre** | Attack, then reinforce the disputed territory before the battle resolves. Legal and strong — you can manoeuvre *into* a dispute, never out |
| **Manoeuvre → Expand** | Consolidate a big stack, then hit with all of it |
| **Tax → Expand** | Bank income while grabbing an empty city |

**Costs:** Footman 1 · Archer 2 · Cavalry 3 · Siege Weapon 10 · Castle 12 ·
Crown Card 10

______________________________________________________________________

## First round, concretely

Open **Tax → Spend** unless there's an unclaimed city adjacent to your starting
stack — an empty city is a free crown *plus* its tax value in coins, and it will
not be there on round three.

Then, in rough priority:

- **Footmen.** They're the dice engine in the general attack and cost 1.
  New players overbuy specialists.
- **A castle (12)** on a border territory.
  Castle territories cannot be attacked at all unless the attacker brings a Siege
  Weapon, and a Siege Weapon costs 10.
  That's the strongest defensive purchase in the game and the one first-timers
  skip.
- **Crown cards (10)** are a crown with no fighting, one per round, only eight in
  the game.
  Two of them is nearly a third of the win condition.
- **Skip Siege Weapons early.** At 10 coins they're a mid-game key for cracking
  castles, not an opener.

______________________________________________________________________

## The teach, in three beats

**1 · Teach the round structure before anything else.** Simultaneous secret
stacking, two turns, then battles.
Do a dry round where everyone just names their two orders out loud so the
sequencing lands.
Don't touch combat yet.

**2 · Teach Expand and Tax only.** Those two carry the first round.
Mention Manoeuvre when someone tries to move inside their own kingdom and
discovers Expand won't do it.

**3 · Teach combat when the first battle actually happens.** Not before.
Walk the ranked order live with dice in hand, because the general attack is the
only genuinely fiddly rule in the box and it makes sense instantly when you're
looking at real units.

## Combat reference

Both sides roll **simultaneously**, in this order, repeating the whole sequence
until one army is gone.

| Rank | Unit | Dice | Hits on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Siege Weapons | 2 per weapon | 3+ |
| 2 | Archers | 1 per archer | 5+ |
| 3 | Cavalry | 1 per cavalry | 3+ |
| 4 | General Attack | see below | compare highest |

Ranks 1–3:
every die at or above the number is an unpreventable hit.
Remove casualties before moving to the next rank.
Only one side rolls a rank if only that side has those units.

**General Attack.** Attacker rolls 3 dice with 3+ units, 2 dice with 2, 1 die
with 1.
Defender rolls 2 dice with 2+ units, 1 die with 1.
Compare the highest die from each side — higher scores a hit.
Discard that pair, compare the next highest pair if both rolled two or more.
**Ties always go to the defender.** Maximum two hits per general attack.

If both armies survive, start again at Rank 1.

**If both sides have only footmen, skip Ranks 1–3 entirely** and just repeat the
General Attack.

## Edge cases — reference only, don't teach these

| Situation | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Expanding into an occupied territory | It becomes **disputed**. Battle resolves at end of round. No retreating, ever |
| Expanding into a castle territory | Not allowed unless your expanding army includes a **Siege Weapon** |
| Third player wants into a dispute | Forbidden, for any reason. Only two armies per disputed territory |
| Expanding out of your own disputed territory | Only the **defender** may, and must leave units equal to the attacker's count |
| Manoeuvring out of a dispute | Never. Committed units cannot retreat |
| Manoeuvre range | Up to 2 territories, through your own supply lines. Stops immediately on entering a disputed territory |
| Leaving a territory empty | Fine for non-city territories. City and castle territories must keep 1 unit |
| Tax and disputes | Disputed territories pay nothing **and sever supply lines**, potentially splitting your kingdom. Pick which city you tax carefully |
| Castle in a disputed territory | Defender re-rolls all dice of one rank, each time the ranked order repeats |
| Both armies wiped out simultaneously | Territory becomes unclaimed, crowns return to the reserve |
| Buying units | Only into city or castle territories you control, never into a dispute |
| Castles | Can't be destroyed, only captured. One per territory. When the reserve runs out, no more get built |
| Crown cards | One purchase per player per round, even with two Spend orders. Only 8 exist |
| King Me bonus | Whoever reveals it takes the First Player Marker; last one to reveal it that round keeps it and starts the next |
| Siege Assault bonus | 2 dice per Siege Weapon, hits on 3+, **no defence dice**. Land only, not from or into disputes |
| Fortify bonus | +3 footmen to a city or +4 to a castle you control. In a dispute, only if you're defending |
| Card you can't use | Still discards at end of round |
| Two players reach 7+ crowns together | Most territories wins, then most coins. First Player Marker counts as 10 coins for that tiebreak |
| Player loses all cities | One grace round to retake one if they hold a crown card, otherwise eliminated. No taxing meanwhile |
