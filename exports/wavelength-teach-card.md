# Wavelength — Teach Card

**Card in play:
Round ←→ Pointy** · Positions on a 0–10 scale (0 = fully Round, 10 = fully
Pointy)

## Setup (30 seconds, say it while you do it)

Two teams.
**Second team starts at 1 point** — it offsets going second.
Pick a psychic each.
Device in the tray, card in the slot.
Done.

______________________________________________________________________

## Prepared clue ladder

Spin, peek at the target, find the nearest entry, say it out loud, say nothing
else.

| Target | Clue |
|:---:|---|
| 0.3 | A ball bearing |
| 1.4 | An orange |
| 2.5 | A hen's egg |
| 3.8 | A teardrop |
| 5.0 | A guitar pick |
| 6.8 | A sharpened pencil |
| 8.4 | An arrowhead |
| 9.7 | A sewing needle |

**The idea to voice out loud:** a clue names a *thing*, never a position.
The skill is picking an object whose roundness matches the target's roundness.

- **1.4 "An orange"** — round, but a stem and a dimple.
  That imperfection is the whole gap between it and a ball bearing at 0.3.
- **5.0 "A guitar pick"** — genuinely half-and-half.
  The dead-centre trap is going vague ("a shape") instead of specifically both.
- **8.4 "An arrowhead"** — pointy on purpose, but with width behind the tip.
  A needle overshoots, a pencil undershoots.
- **Deliberate near-miss to demo:** for 6.8, "a knife" reads as 8-plus.
  People picture the tip, not the blade.

## The teach, in three beats

**1 · Before you spin.** "There's a hidden target on this line.
Only I can see it.
I'll name one thing — you move the dial to where you think my target is."
Spin, peek, close the screen, give the clue.
Explain nothing else.

Let them argue.
Let them set the dial.
*Then* open the screen and show the rings — 2, 3, **4**, 3, 2 — and let them
discover what they scored.
Scoring lands better as a reveal than a preamble.

**2 · The other team's point.** Second team's turn.
Stop them the moment the dial locks:
"Before we reveal — other team, bet whether the true centre is **left** or
**right** of that dial.
Correct = 1 point."
This is the rule everyone forgets, so introduce it exactly when it goes live.

**3 · What you couldn't have said.** Only now, after two clean rounds, cover the
clue rules below.
Framed as "here's what I was avoiding," they land as craft instead of legalese.

## Clue rules

**Illegal**

- Card words *or their synonyms* — "spherical," "circular," "sharp," "jagged"
- Numbers and positions — "seven out of ten," "a third in from the left"
- Directional comparisons — "rounder than a football" (that's a vector, not a
  point)
- Two clues — "an egg… actually more like an almond."
  One concept, then silence.
- Everything after the clue — no gestures, no wincing, no answering questions.
  Poker face until the screen opens.

**Legal but wastes your turn**

- Bare adjectives with no anchor:
  "smooth," "dangerous"
- Private knowledge:
  "Dave's nose"
- Abstractions on a physical card:
  "jazz" — fine later, noise here

**Note the asymmetry:** the numbers ban applies *only to the psychic's clue*.
The guessing team should absolutely say "an egg feels like a 2.5" while they
debate.
That's the game working.

## Edge cases — reference only, don't teach these

| Situation | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Dial lands on a line between wedges | Psychic's team takes the **higher** value |
| Psychic's team hits the bullseye (4) | Other team scores **nothing**, even with a correct left/right bet |
| Dial exactly on target centre | No paradox — it's a bullseye, so the rule above covers it |
| 4 points scored *and still behind* | Immediate extra turn, **different psychic**. Can chain — 9–1 is winnable |
| Dial misses the target entirely | Other team still collects their left/right point |
| Psychic hates the spectrum | Either side of the card is fair game. One redraw allowed, but only *before* the spin |
| Team deadlocks on the dial | Tiebreaker decides — nominate next round's psychic at setup |
| Both teams cross 10 in one round | Play another round until someone is clearly ahead |
| Timer | None in the box. Add 1 min psychic / 2 min team only if one specific person stalls |
| 2–3 players | Drop the left/right phase, play fully co-op for a group high score |

**First to 10 wins.**
