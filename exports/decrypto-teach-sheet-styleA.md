# Decrypto — Teach Sheet

*Style A.
Quotes are said aloud, brackets are done.
Read top to bottom.*

______________________________________________________________________

## FRONT

**1** [Deal one screen per team]

**[4+ players]** "Two teams.
Four secret words behind each screen.
Every round one of you sends your own team a three-digit code using clues — and
the other team is listening in.
Intercept them twice and you win.
Fail to understand your own encryptor twice and you lose."

**[3 players]** "Two of you are a team, one of you is the interceptor.
The pair sends each other coded messages; you try to crack them.
Two successful intercepts inside five rounds and you win — otherwise the pair
does."

______________________________________________________________________

**2** [Slot keyword cards in, hand out note sheets]

"Behind your screen:
four words, numbered one to four.
The other team never sees them.

Two things to settle before we start.
Clues can be a single word or a short phrase — pick one and we all stick to it.
And decide whether proper nouns are allowed.
Both work, they just have to match."

______________________________________________________________________

**3** "Here's the part everyone gets wrong.
Your team is not guessing the words.
They already know the words.
They're guessing the **numbers** — which of your four words you're pointing at,
and in what order."

______________________________________________________________________

**4** [Encryptor draws a code card.
Start the sand timer.]

"Draw a code — three digits.
Write one clue for each digit, in that order.
Then read all three aloud.

Everyone guessing writes their three numbers down **privately first**, then you
compare and agree as a team.° Encryptor rotates every round, so everyone takes a
turn in the chair."

[Encryptor reads.
Team guesses.
Reveal.]

"Nobody intercepted that one — nothing was written down yet.
That changes now."

______________________________________________________________________

**5** [Top of round two.
Point at the note sheets.]

"Every clue from last round is on paper now, permanently, on both sheets.
From here on the *other* team writes their guess and reveals it **before** your
team answers.

Right if they're right, that's an interception.
Wrong costs them nothing — they get a free attempt every round for the rest of
the game."

______________________________________________________________________

*° House convention, not in the rulebook:
stops the loudest player steering the team into a wrong answer.*

## BACK

### These five answer almost every question

**Success earns nothing.** You're only ever avoiding penalties, never scoring.

**Intercepting is free.** A wrong intercept costs the interceptor nothing, every
round, forever.

**Clues describe meaning, never spelling.** No letter counts, no positions, no
rhymes.

**The keyword is never a clue,** in any form.

**Every round needs new clues.** No reuse, ever.

### Genuine exceptions — all of them endgame only

**Both conditions at once.** A team can hit two intercepts *and* two
miscommunications in the same round.
Go to the tiebreak.

**Both teams at once.** Same — go to the tiebreak.

**The tiebreak.** Intercept +1, miscommunication −1.
Higher total wins.

**Still tied.** Each team names the opponents' four words.
Most correct wins.

**Round 8.** The sheet has eight rows and that's the whole game.
If nobody has won, use the tiebreak.

### Uneven teams

Fine as they are.
The bigger team is arguably worse off — more voices to talk each other out of
the right answer.

# Format demo — Risk: Europe opening

*Showing the player-count branch on a game where it actually bites.
Read one line, skip the other.*

**1**
[Everyone takes an army, war banner, 8 King's Orders cards, 5 silver, 1 crown, 1 castle]

"You're medieval monarchs.
Crowns win the game — you get them by holding cities, taking other people's
cities, or buying them outright."

**[4 players]** "Hold **seven** crowns at the end of any round and you win."
**[3 players]** "Hold **⟨check rulebook⟩** crowns at the end of any round and
you win."
**[2 players]** "Two-player has its own adjustments — read the back page of the
rulebook before you set up."

______________________________________________________________________

**2** "Bid for who goes first.
Hide however many of your five coins you want in your fist, all reveal at once.
Highest bid pays it and goes first.
Everyone else keeps their money."

"Now pick a Gold Crown City.
Crown and castle go there, you collect its bonus tile and its tax value in
coins, and ten footmen split between that city and one territory next to it."

**[Rome]** "Rome starts you one space further up the crown track."

______________________________________________________________________

**3** "Don't read your cards to me and I won't read them to you.
Look at your eight order cards now — each has two options, they say what they
do.
Ask me about anything unclear, then we start."

*This step replaces roughly four minutes of explanation.
The cards are legible; narrating them adds load rather than removing it.*
