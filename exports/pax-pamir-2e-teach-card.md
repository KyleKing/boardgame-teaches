# Pax Pamir 2e — Teach Card

1–5 players · Win with a **4+ point lead after any Dominance Check**, or the
most points after the **fourth and final Check**

## Setup essentials

Each player:
11 cylinders (one to the 0 space of the VP track), a loyalty dial, a player
board, and **4 rupees**.
Market is 12 cards in **2 rows × 6 columns**, filled leftmost column first, top
row first.
**The favoured suit starts on Political.**

The deck is built so the **four Dominance Check cards sit in the bottom four
piles** — the game has a known, finite length, and everyone should know that
from turn one.

______________________________________________________________________

## The loop

**Up to two actions per turn.** Fewer is allowed.
Then cleanup, then clockwise.

Your three action types:

- **Purchase** — take a card from the market into hand.
  Cost = its column number; leftmost column is **free**.
  Pay by putting 1 rupee on each card to its left *in the same row*.
  You collect any rupees already sitting on the card you buy.
  You may not buy a card you put a rupee on this turn.
- **Play** — move a card from hand into your court, at the left or right edge.
  Its impact icons fire immediately.
- **Card action** — use one action on one court card:
  Tax, Gift, Build, Move, Betray, Battle.
  **One action per card per turn**, no matter how many icons it shows.

**The rule that makes the game:** actions on cards matching the **favoured suit
are free** — they don't count against your two.
A court stacked with the favoured suit takes five or six actions a turn.
This is the single largest swing available to you, and it's why suit-changing
cards get fought over.

**When Military is favoured, all purchase costs double.**

______________________________________________________________________

## Suits and stars

| Suit | What its stars do |
|---|---|
| **Political** | Court size = 3 + your political stars |
| **Intelligence** | Hand size = 2 + your intelligence stars |
| **Economic** | Protect your rupees from being taxed |
| **Military** | Final tiebreaker only |

Both limits apply **at end of turn** — mid-turn you can exceed them freely.

______________________________________________________________________

## Your first few turns, concretely

**Turn 1 is forced and that's fine.** You have 4 rupees and an empty court, so
you have no card actions available.
The only sensible turn is **Purchase + Play.** Take the free leftmost card if
it's usable; pay 1–2 rupees for a better one.

What to want in that first card, in order:

1. **An action you'll reuse** — Build, Tax, or Gift.
   A card whose only value is its one-time impact icon is a bad first pick.
1. **Political suit or political stars.** The favoured suit starts Political, so
   those actions are free right now, and political stars raise your court cap so
   the engine can keep growing.
1. **A region you can commit to.** Region matters more than new players expect —
   see the coupling rule below.

**Turns 2 and 3:
keep building the court.** A one- or two-card court has almost no options, and
in this game options *are* the position.
Resist the urge to start doing things on the map before you have three or four
cards down.

**Things to hold off on:**

- **Don't lock in loyalty early.** Changing coalitions costs you all gifts and
  prizes — which is free when you have none and brutal later.
  Stay flexible for the first two or three turns.
- **Don't hoard rupees** unless you have economic stars.
  Unprotected money is a target for the Tax action.
- **Watch the Leveraged icon** (the scales).
  It hands you 2 rupees now, but discarding that card later costs 2 rupees, or a
  card per rupee you can't pay.

**The read that actually decides your spending:** *will the next Dominance Check
succeed or fail?* If one coalition is closing on +4 blocks, buy influence —
gifts, patriots.
If the map is balanced and a failure looks likely, put **cylinders** on the
board instead, because failure pays whoever has the most cylinders in play.
New players buy influence for a check that never succeeds and score nothing.

## The teach, in three beats

**1 · Teach only Purchase and Play.** Have everyone take a real turn buying and
playing one card.
Nothing else.
Let the market economy — rupees piling up on skipped cards — be discovered
rather than explained.

**2 · Teach card actions when someone has a court.** Introduce them as "your
court is your action menu," then reveal the favoured-suit bonus.
Watching someone realise they can chain four free actions is the moment the game
clicks.

**3 · Teach Dominance Checks last, before the first one enters the market.**
Explain the two outcomes and then let everyone look at the map and predict which
one is coming.
That single question is the whole strategy layer, and it's meaningless until
there are blocks on the board to look at.

## Dominance Check reference

Triggered when a Dominance Check card would be **discarded from the leftmost
column** or is purchased.
Check whether a single coalition has **at least 4 more blocks on the map** than
each other coalition.

**Success — one coalition dominates.** Only players loyal to it score, ranked by
influence:
**5 / 3 / 1** for most / second / third.
Ties add the contested places together and split them.
Influence = **1 + patriots + gifts + prizes**.
Then **remove all coalition blocks from the map** — an uneasy peace.

**Failure — no coalition dominates.** Everyone scores by **cylinders in play**:
**3** for most, **1** for second.
Blocks stay on the map.

**The final Check scores double.** If two Dominance Check cards are ever in the
market at once, resolve one immediately.

## Edge cases — reference only, don't teach these

| Situation | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Lose your last **tribe** in a region | Immediately discard **all your
political cards** from that region |
| Lose your last **political card** of a region | Immediately remove **all your
tribes** from that region |
| Discarding a **Leveraged** card | Pay 2 rupees, or discard 1 card per rupee
you can't pay |
| Card with a spy gets discarded | Spies return to their owners' supplies |
| Court over the limit at end of turn | Discard down to 3 + political stars.
Same for hand at 2 + intelligence stars |
| Changing loyalty | Lose all gifts and prizes.
Patriots return.
Playing a patriot of another coalition **forces** the change |
| Ruling a region | Most pieces there.
Enemy coalition armies can block you from taking the ruler token |
| Tax | Always available against the market.
Against a *player* only if you rule a region where they hold a court card — and
their economic stars shield their rupees |
| Build | Only in regions you rule.
**2 rupees per unit**, paid to the market |
| Action costs | Paid onto the **rightmost** cards of both market rows, 1 rupee
each.
Any card you put a rupee on is unbuyable this turn |
| Market runs short of cards for payment | Excess rupees leave the game |
| Event card reaches the leftmost column | Discarded during cleanup and its
**top** effect hits everyone.
Bottom effect only fires if a player buys it |
| Rupees on a discarded event | Stay in that position and transfer to whatever
card slides in |
| Final tiebreak | Most military stars, then most rupees |
