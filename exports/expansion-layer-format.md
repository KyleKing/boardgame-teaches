# Expansion layers — format convention

**The problem:** an expansion is never "more rules at the end."
It changes setup, alters existing beats, and sometimes replaces the opponent
entirely.
Appending a page to a teach sheet is therefore wrong — you'd be teaching a base
game nobody is playing, then correcting yourself.

**The fix:** the base spine gets **stable step numbers**, and expansions ship as
**patch sheets keyed to those numbers**.
You lay the patch beside the spine and read them together.
Adding a module never means reprinting the base.

______________________________________________________________________

## Three patch types

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **+4a** | **Insert.** A new beat, slotted after step 4.
Spine numbering unchanged |
| **~4** | **Amend.** Step 4 still happens, but with this line added or changed
|
| **=4** | **Replace.** Step 4 is void.
Read this instead |

## Provenance marker

Because expansion stacks mix official and fan content, and a table deserves to
know which it's playing:

**◆ official** · **◇ fan-made**

# Demo — 7 Wonders Duel

Two players by default, so the branch pattern here isn't player count.
It's **which modules are on the table.** The teacher resolves that once at setup
and then reads a fixed path.

## Base spine (abbreviated to show the keys)

**1** ▸ Goal:
three ways to win — military advance, six science symbols, or most points at the
end of Age III
**2** ▸ Setup:
wonders draft, three age decks, progress tokens
**3** ▸ The card pyramid:
take an uncovered card, build it, discard it for coins, or bury it under a
wonder
**4** ▸ Age transition and who chooses first
**5** ▸ Scoring

______________________________________________________________________

## ◆ Pantheon

**~2** Setup changes, not adds.
Mythology tokens and offerings enter the age setups, so this patches step 2
rather than appending a step.

**+3a** The divinities row:
a parallel economy with its own currency and its own timing.

> This is the case that proves the format.
> Pantheon touches step 2, and if you'd stapled it to the end you would have
> taught a setup that never happens.

______________________________________________________________________

## ◇ Solo

**=1** Goal is restated — you're playing against a Leader with scripted
behaviour, not an opponent making choices.

**=3** The opponent's turn is driven by **Decision cards** rather than
decisions.
This is a replacement, not an amendment:
the core loop is different.

**+2a** Choose a Leader as your opponent.

> Worth saying out loud at the table:
> 7 Wonders Duel's solo mode is a fan print-and-play built around Leaders and
> Decision cards, not an official mode.
> That's not a criticism — it's well-regarded — but a player asking "is this how
> the designer intended it?"
> deserves a straight answer.

______________________________________________________________________

**+2a′** Three additional Leaders available as opponents:
<cite index="106-1">Caligula, Sappho, and Imhotep, who use progress tokens and
can take extra turns.</cite>

**~2a** <cite index="106-1">Some Decision cards are updated when Pantheon is

______________________________________________________________________

## ◇ Solo + Agora

**+0** No new Leaders.
<cite index="105-1">Neither of Agora's two progress tokens has any effect for
the Leader, so this layer adds none</cite> — but base and Pantheon Leaders <cite
index="105-1">interact with the senate and decrees in new ways.</cite>

**~2a** Interaction patch only:
the Leader's turn is <cite index="105-1">modified to allow taking senator cards
and placing influence cubes, Decision cards can offer senators or direct cube
placement, and military tokens affect cube placement.</cite>

______________________________________________________________________

## ◇ Solo + Pantheon + Agora together

**~2** The one genuinely combinatorial patch — a setup line that exists *only*
when both modules are present.
<cite index="105-1">Merge the Decision cards from both expansions:
you should end with 3 carrying the Pantheon icon, 5 carrying the Agora icon, and
4 with no icon.</cite>

> **The lesson for the format.** Patches don't compose cleanly.
> A rule can exist only at the intersection of two modules, which means the patch
> sheet needs its own combination section rather than assuming the reader can
> stack two independent sheets.
> Name the combinations you actually own; don't try to be general.

## Caveat

The specific patch contents above are illustrative and drawn from secondary
sources — verify each against your own copies before printing.
The **keying convention** is the deliverable here; the 7WD line items are the
worked example.
