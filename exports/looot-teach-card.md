# Looot — Teach Card

*Gigamic, 2024* · 2–4 players · Game ends when everyone has placed all **13
Vikings**

## Setup

One landscape board per player, slotted together.
Place **2 Houses, 2 Watchtowers, 3 Castles** on their marked spaces.
Trophy board along one edge with **5 Trophies in ascending order**.
Ocean board along another edge:
30 Longships in the bag, draw 5 face up.

Each player:
1 Fjord board, **13 Vikings**, **3 Shields**, and one each of Port / Altar /
Jarl Palace construction site tiles, face up on their Fjord.
Resource reserve (Axes, Wood, Sheep, Gold) beside the board.
Player who lives furthest north starts.

______________________________________________________________________

## The loop

**Mandatory every turn:** place one Viking on the shared board.
It must be **adjacent to any Viking of any colour, or to a Longship**, and not
on a resource space another Viking already occupies.
Then take that resource type from the reserve and place it on an empty space in
**your** Fjord.
It can never be moved again.

Then check whether you captured a building, and whether you completed a
construction site.

**Optional, at end of turn:** take one Longship, use a Shield, claim a Trophy.

**The double-bookkeeping to flag immediately:** Vikings go on the *shared* map,
everything they earn goes on *your private* Fjord.
New players keep trying to build in one place.

______________________________________________________________________

## Capturing buildings

| Building | How |
|---|---|
| **House** | Place a Viking adjacent to it → take 1 tile |
| **Watchtower** | Connect **two** watchtowers with a chain of *your* Vikings → 1 tile for **each**. Recapturable, but only by connecting to a **different** watchtower |
| **Castle** | Chain **4+** of your Vikings with at least one adjacent to a castle → 1 tile. **8** in the chain → a second. **12** → a third. Each extra tile needs a genuinely longer chain |

## The scoring, which is the entire game

Each type scores **(base value + your longship bonuses for that type) × how many
you own.**

The rulebook's own example:
castles have a base of 4.
With two castle bonuses of +3 and +4, that's 11 — times **3** castles = **33
points**.

**Say this before turn one, because it changes every decision:** it's
multiplicative.
A bonus is worthless without quantity, and quantity is worth little without
bonuses.
Spreading yourself across all six scoring types gives you small numbers times
small numbers.
Pick two, maybe three, and stack.

Then add completed construction sites and your trophy, and **subtract 5 for
every Longship you didn't fill.**

**Axes score nothing on their own.** They're trophy currency and construction
material.
This is the most commonly misvalued tile in the box.

## Opening guidance

- **Fjord space is the real constraint,** not Vikings.
  Every tile is permanent, and construction sites and longships need specific
  things *adjacent* to them.
  Look at your Fjord's geometry before your first placement, not after your fifth.
- **Decide early whether you're a castle player.** A 12-Viking chain is nearly
  your entire supply of 13.
  That's a whole-game commitment, and it can't be a mid-game pivot.
- **Longships are contracts, not gifts.** Minus 5 is enormous.
  Never take one you can't see three specific resources for.
- **Houses are the reliable engine** — bare adjacency, no chain-building.
  Watchtowers need you to scout which *pairs* are reachable, since each repeat
  capture needs a new partner tower.
- **Spend your Shields.** Three one-shots:
  place a second Viking, place onto an occupied space, or take 2 resources instead
  of 1.
  Dying with unused shields is the most common first-game mistake.
- **Trophies are ascending and one per player, ever.** Waiting means a worse one
  or none.

## The teach, in three beats

**1 · Teach the placement rule alone.** Place a Viking adjacent to another
Viking, take the resource, put it on your Fjord.
Have everyone do one turn.
Say nothing about buildings.

**2 · Teach capture when someone's placement triggers it.** Houses first, since
they'll happen naturally.
Introduce watchtowers and castles as "the same idea, but you need a chain" —
chains are much easier to grasp with Vikings already on the board than as an
abstraction.

**3 · Teach the scoring formula before anyone takes a Longship.** This is the
one thing that genuinely must come early rather than late, because the
multiplication is what makes a longship worth taking and a −5 worth fearing.
Walk the castle example out loud with real tiles.

## Edge cases — reference only, don't teach these

| Situation | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Your Fjord is full | You still must place Vikings, but you **cannot take any more tiles**. Watch for this — it can arrive early and quietly |
| Placing next to an opponent's Viking | Always legal. Any colour counts for adjacency |
| Placing adjacent to a Longship | Legal — the Ocean board counts as adjacency for placement |
| Occupied resource space | Can't place there, unless you spend a Shield |
| Resource reserve runs out | Take whatever remains |
| Capturing the same watchtower twice | Only by connecting it to a **different** watchtower |
| Second and third castle tiles | Require a **longer** chain each time — 8, then 12. Not a repeat of the same chain |
| Longship selection | One per turn, at end of turn. Draw a replacement into the Ocean immediately |
| Filling a Longship | The 3 shown resources must be **adjacent** to it on your Fjord. Flip it for the bonus |
| Unfilled Longship | **−5 each** at game end |
| Construction sites | Required resources and buildings adjacent in any arrangement, then flip |
| Trophy | Needs at least as many **Axes** as shown. **One per player per game**, no exceptions |
| Tiles placed on your Fjord | Never move again. Ever |
| Tie at game end | Highest-value Trophy wins. No trophy on either side → shared victory |
