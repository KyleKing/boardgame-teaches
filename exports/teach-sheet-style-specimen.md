# Teach Sheet — Style Specimen

**Test game:
Decrypto.** Same content, three scripting styles, so you can compare like for
like.
Front side is one of A/B/C.
Back side is settled and shown once.

One structural change from the earlier cards:
**the win/lose condition now comes first.** Teaching backwards from the goal is
the strongest consensus in the research, and the old version deliberately
withheld it.
The fix is to state the *goal* immediately but defer the *mechanism* —
interception still doesn't get explained until round two.

Step count reduced from 6 to 5.
Nothing about screens, keyword slots, or the note sheet is explained, because
those are self-evident once they're in someone's hands.

# STYLE A — Verbatim

*Everything in quotes is said aloud.
Everything in brackets is done, not said.*

______________________________________________________________________

**1** [Deal screens, one per team]

"Two teams.
Each team has four secret words behind that screen.
Every round, one of you sends your team a three-digit code using clues — and the
other team is listening.
Intercept their code twice and you win.
Fail to understand your own encryptor twice and you lose."

**2** [Slot keyword cards in, hand out note sheets]

"Look behind your screen.
Four words, numbered one to four.
The other team never sees them."

**3** "Here's the part everyone gets wrong the first time.
Your team is not guessing the words.
They already know the words.
They're guessing the **numbers** — which of your four words you're pointing at,
and in what order."

**4** [Encryptor draws a code card]

"Draw a code.
Three digits.
Write one clue for each digit, in that order, then read the three clues aloud.
Your team writes down the three numbers they think you mean."

[Encryptor reads.
Team guesses.
Reveal.]

"Nobody intercepted that one, because nothing was written down yet.
That changes now."

**5** [At the top of round two, point at the note sheets]

"Every clue from last round is on paper, permanently, on both sheets.
From now on the *other* team writes their guess and reveals it **before** your
team gets to answer.
If they're right, that's an interception.
If they're wrong, it costs them nothing — they get a free attempt every single
round for the rest of the game."

# STYLE B — Beat sheet

*Bold is said close to verbatim.
Everything else is yours to phrase.*

______________________________________________________________________

**1 · Frame the two failure modes** — teams, secret words, coded messages.
**"Intercept them twice and you win.
Misunderstand your own encryptor twice and you lose."**

**2 · Words behind screens, numbered 1–4.** Hand out note sheets, say nothing
else about them.

**3 · The one line that must land:**
**"Your team isn't guessing the words.
They're guessing the numbers."**

**4 · Play round one exposed.** Draw code → three clues in digit order → read
aloud → team guesses → reveal.
Close with:
**"Nobody intercepted, because nothing was on paper yet."**

**5 · Round two, point at the sheets.** Introduce interception here, not
earlier.
**"They guess and reveal before your team answers.
A wrong intercept costs them nothing."**

# BACK SIDE — grouped by phase

*Same for all three styles.
Scan the phase heading, not the whole sheet.*

## Setup

- Round 1 — no interception, either team
- Sand timer is in the box · use it on the encryptor only
- Agree now:
  single words or short phrases · proper nouns in or out

## Giving clues

- One clue per digit, **in code order**
- Meaning only — never spelling, letter position, or rhyme
- Never the keyword or any form of it
- New clues every round · no reuse
- Encryptor rotates each round

## Guessing

- Opponents commit and reveal **first**
- Wrong intercept → nothing
- Own team correct → nothing, just no penalty
- Own team wrong → miscommunication

## Tokens and ending

- 2 intercepts → win · 2 miscommunications → lose
- Both at once, or both teams at once → tiebreak
- Tiebreak → intercept +1, miscommunication −1
- Still tied → name the opponents' four words, most correct wins
- Nobody won by round 8 → sheet is full, go to tiebreak

## Player counts

- 3 players → 2 v 1.
  Solo interceptor also takes a token when the pair miscommunicates.
  Needs 2 within 5 rounds or the pair wins
- Uneven teams → fine.
  Larger team is arguably worse off
