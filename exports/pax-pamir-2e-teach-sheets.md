# SHEET 1 · FRONT — The spine

## 1 ▸ Goal and length, before anything else

- Afghan leaders after the Durrani collapse; Britain and Russia are meddling
- Points come from **Dominance Checks**.
  There are four, seeded in the deck
- The fourth one ends the game and scores double

`"Two ways this ends. Lead everyone by four points after any check and you win
on the spot. Otherwise it runs to the fourth check and most points wins."`

______________________________________________________________________

## 2 ▸ Pick a loyalty

- Three coalitions:
  British, Russian, Afghan
- [Each player chooses, starting with a random player]

`"Switching sides later costs you every gift and prize you're holding. Right now
you're holding none — so it's free today and brutal in twenty minutes."`

______________________________________________________________________

## 3 ▸ The market — do this live, don't describe it

[Everyone takes a real turn here.
Purchase, then play.]

- Two rows, six columns.
  **Leftmost column is free**
- Cost = column number, paid as one rupee onto each card to its left *in the same
  row*
- You collect any rupees already sitting on the card you take
- Playing a card puts it in your **court** and fires its impact icons immediately

`"You can't buy a card you just put a rupee on. That's this turn only."`

______________________________________________________________________

## 4 ▸ Your court is your action menu — and the free-action reveal

- Two actions a turn.
  Purchase, play, or use a card action
- Card actions:
  Tax, Gift, Build, Move, Betray, Battle
- **One action per card per turn**, however many icons it shows

`"Now the important part. One suit is always favoured — it starts on Political.
Actions on cards matching the favoured suit are free. They don't count against
your two."`

- So a stacked court takes five or six actions a turn
- Which is why the cards that *change* the favoured suit get fought over
- `"And when Military is favoured, every card in the market costs double."`

______________________________________________________________________

## 5 ▸ The map, and the rule that couples it to your cards

- Cylinders are **tribes** (yours) · blocks are **armies** in regions, **roads**
  on borders
- Rule a region = most pieces there.
  Ruling gates Build and lets you Tax players
- Build costs 2 rupees per unit

`"These two are a pair, and they're how people attack you. Lose your last tribe
in a region and every political card you hold from that region is discarded.
Lose your last political card from a region and all your tribes there come off
the board."`

______________________________________________________________________

## 6 ▸ Dominance Checks — the standing question

- Fires when a Check card is bought, or discarded off the leftmost column
- **Dominant** = one coalition has 4+ more blocks than each other coalition

**If a coalition dominates:** only players loyal to it score, **5 / 3 / 1** by
influence.
Influence = 1 + patriots + gifts + prizes.
Then every block comes off the map.

**If none dominates:** everyone scores by cylinders on the board, **3** for
most, **1** for second.
Blocks stay.

`"So before you spend anything, look at the map and ask which of those two is
coming. They reward opposite things. People lose this game buying influence for
a check that never succeeds."`

# SHEET 1 · BACK — First three turns

*Read this out after step 6.
It's coaching, which is normally bad practice — but Pax Pamir's opening is
genuinely non-obvious and a wasted first three turns is a lost game.*

## Turn 1 is forced, and that's fine

`"You have four rupees and an empty court, so you have no actions available.
Everyone's first turn is buy a card, then play it. That's it."`

**What to want in that first card, in order:**

- An action you'll reuse — Build, Tax, or Gift.
  A card that's only a one-time impact icon is a bad first pick
- Political suit or political stars — Political is favoured right now, so those
  actions are free, and the stars raise your court cap
- A region you're willing to commit to

## Turns 2 and 3 ▸ keep building the court

- A two-card court has almost no options, and options *are* the position
- Resist doing things on the map before you have three or four cards down

## Three traps

- **Don't lock loyalty early.** Free to switch now, expensive later
- **Don't hoard rupees** without economic stars.
  Unprotected money gets taxed
- **The scales icon** hands you 2 rupees and marks the card Leveraged — discarding
  it later costs 2 rupees back

# SHEET 2 · FRONT — Rules that answer most questions

**Rupees are never safe** unless you have economic stars.

**One action per card per turn,** no matter how many icons it has.

**Any card you put a rupee on is unbuyable until next turn.**

**Both hand and court limits only apply at end of turn.** Mid-turn, exceed them
freely.

**Playing a patriot of another coalition switches your loyalty** whether you
meant it or not.

**Tribes and cards of a region live or die together.** Lose the last of either
and you lose the other.

**A successful check wipes the map.** Everything you built in blocks is gone;
influence survives.

# SHEET 2 · BACK — Exceptions, by phase

## Buying and playing

- Market can't absorb the rupees you owe → excess leaves the game
- Event card reaches the leftmost column → discarded in cleanup, its **top**
  effect hits everyone.
  The bottom effect only fires if someone buys it
- Rupees on a discarded event → stay in that position, transfer to whatever card
  slides in
- Two Check cards in the market at once → resolve one immediately

## Using card actions

- Action costs pay onto the **rightmost** cards of both rows, one rupee each
- Tax a *player* → only if you rule a region where they hold a court card
- Build → only in regions you rule
- Ruler token → enemy coalition armies can block you from taking it

## Discarding

- Over court limit → down to 3 + political stars · over hand limit → down to 2 +
  intelligence stars
- Leveraged card → 2 rupees, or one card per rupee you can't pay
- Card carrying spies → spies go home to their owners

## Scoring

- Influence ties → add the contested places together and divide
- Final check → doubles
- Final tiebreak → most military stars, then most rupees

# SHEET 3 — Player reference

*Print one per player.
This one is not teacher-facing — it stays on the table.*

## Two actions a turn

**Purchase** · **Play** · **Card action** (Tax, Gift, Build, Move, Betray,
Battle)

Favoured-suit actions are **free**.
One action per card per turn.

## Stars

| Suit | Effect |
|---|---|
| Political | Court size = 3 + stars |
| Intelligence | Hand size = 2 + stars |
| Economic | Rupees can't be taxed |
| Military | Final tiebreaker |

## Costs

Card = its column number · Build = 2 rupees per unit · Gifts = 2 / 4 / 6

## Influence

**1 + patriots + gifts + prizes**

## Dominance Check

**One coalition 4+ blocks ahead** → loyal players score **5 / 3 / 1** by
influence → then all blocks come off the map

**Nobody 4+ ahead** → everyone scores by cylinders on the map, **3 / 1** →
blocks stay

Fourth check is the last, and it doubles.
