# Decrypto — Teach Card

3–8 players, two teams · Win at **2 Interception tokens** · Lose at **2
Miscommunication tokens** · 8 rounds max

## Setup (1 minute)

Two teams.
Each takes a screen, 4 keyword cards slotted in behind it (positions **1–4**), a
code deck, and a note sheet.
Sand timer and tokens in the middle.
Only your own team sees your keywords.

**Say this once, clearly:** your team never guesses the *words*.
They guess the *numbers*.

______________________________________________________________________

## The loop

Each round, both teams' Encryptors draw a code card — three of the digits 1–4,
in order, e.g. **4-1-2**.
Write one clue per digit, in order, then read them aloud.

1. Opposing team writes an interception guess and reveals **first**.
1. Your own team then gives their guess.
1. Reveal the code.

Correct interception → they take an **Interception token**.
Your team wrong → you take a **Miscommunication token**.
A wrong interception costs nothing.
A correct own-team guess earns nothing — it just avoids the penalty.

______________________________________________________________________

## The worked example — this is the whole teach

Keywords:
**1 BEACH · 2 MOON · 3 SPIDER · 4 ENGINE**

**Round 1, code 4-1-2.** Nobody can intercept in round one, so obvious is
*correct*:

> PISTON · SAND · CRATER

**Round 3, code 1-2-3.** The opponents now have SAND written under 1 and CRATER
under 2.
Repeating that family hands them the round:

> NORMANDY · WEREWOLF · WEB

Beach becomes a D-Day landing.
Moon becomes a werewolf trigger.
Spider is fresh, so WEB is still safe — first clues for a number can be direct.

**Round 5, code 2-3-1.** Moon now shows CRATER + WEREWOLF; spider shows WEB.
Two data points is enough for opponents to triangulate, so this is where it gets
genuinely hard:

> CHEESE · TARANTINO · CANCÚN

**The pattern to name out loud:** the *second* clue you give for a number is the
dangerous one.
One clue is ambiguous; two clues intersect.
Round one you're helping your team.
By round four you're mostly hiding from the other table.

______________________________________________________________________

## The teach, in three beats

**1 · Round one, no interception.** Run it without mentioning interception at
all.
Just:
"Here's a code.
Write a clue for each digit.
Read them out."
Your team guesses, you reveal.
Everyone understands the core in ninety seconds.

**2 · Introduce interception at the top of round two.** Now point at their note
sheets:
"Everything said last round is written down and permanent.
From now on, the other team guesses your code *before* your team does."
The dread lands correctly here — after they've already given away three easy
clues.

**3 · The two tokens.** Only now explain that two interceptions win and two
miscommunications lose.
Late is better, because people immediately grasp why:
you're being squeezed from both sides, and the safe play and the winning play
point in opposite directions.

## Clue rules

**Illegal**

- The keyword itself, or any form or part of it
- Clues about **spelling or letter position** — "starts with B," "five letters,"
  "rhymes with peach."
  Clues must attach to the word's *meaning*
- Reusing a clue you've already given — every round needs new clues
- Anything outside the language you agreed on at setup

**Legal but loses games**

- Inside jokes and private references.
  The rulebook forbids them, and they also make your own team's later rounds
  impossible when the joke runs dry.
- Clues that are clever but only work backwards — if you have to explain it after
  the reveal, it failed.
- Being too safe.
  A team that never takes a miscommunication risk gets intercepted twice and
  loses.

**Agree at the table before round one:** single words or short phrases, and
whether proper nouns are in.
Both are common; what matters is that both teams play the same way.

## Edge cases — reference only, don't teach these

| Situation | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Round 1 | Neither team may intercept.
No information exists yet |
| Interception timing | Opponents commit and reveal **before** the active team
guesses |
| Wrong interception | No penalty.
Interception is a free roll every round |
| Own team guesses right | No reward — you simply avoid the token |
| Encryptor | Rotates every round within the team |
| Both teams hit 2 interceptions in the same round | Tiebreak, below |
| One team hits 2 interceptions *and* 2 miscommunications | Also a tiebreak —
you can win and lose at once |
| Tiebreak | Interception +1, Miscommunication −1, higher total wins |
| Still tied | Each team names the opponent's four keywords.
Most correct wins |
| Nobody has won by round 8 | Note sheet is full — go to the tiebreak above |
| Timer | Sand timer is in the box.
Use it on the Encryptor, not on the guessers |
| 3 players | 2 vs 1.
The solo Interceptor takes a token when the pair miscommunicates, and must reach
2 tokens within 5 rounds or the pair wins |
| Odd player counts | Uneven teams are fine.
The larger team is arguably disadvantaged — more voices to talk each other out
of the right answer |
