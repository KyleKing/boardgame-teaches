# 5 · Interactive teaching: rolling teaches, tutorial campaigns, and the TTRPG pattern

The teach-up-front and the teach-in-play are the two poles. This chapter collects the best examples of the second pole and the argument between them.

## The Rolling Teach: John Company 2e

The canonical source is the BGG thread [Script for rolling teach](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2960133/script-for-rolling-teach) (October 2022) and its companion [Things to highlight before the rolling teach](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2959397/things-to-highlight-rolling-teach). The method, assembled from the thread:

- "The script is on the board, just follow the ribbon round" (Ben Goulding). John Company prints its phase sequence as a ribbon around the board edge, so the teach order is the play order. The board is the teach sheet
- Skip what round 1 doesn't use: "London Season and Hiring can be skipped in Round 1, so don't even bother talking about those." They get taught at the top of round 2, so the teach literally rolls across two rounds, matching [Cole Wehrle's own teach-and-play video](https://boardgamegeek.com/video/313774/john-company-second-edition/john-company-2nd-edition-teach-and-play-feat-cole)
- The teacher takes the load-bearing seats (Chairman and Prime Minister) for round 1 "so you can talk the players through what you're doing and why so that they have an example to follow"
- One deliberate pre-load: explain the Presidents' Trade action before starting, because "Most of the prior office actions are building up to this action." Without it you keep saying "I'll come back to why," which hijacks the rolling structure (Jonny Newcombe, crediting the Wehrlegig Discord)
- Frame-setting before the roll starts. From Cole's video, transcribed by a thread participant: "this is a business game in the 18th century. Ships sank, plans went awry… This means… we're rolling dice." Set the mindset before the first catastrophic roll, not after
- Expectation-setting is part of the teach: "players should be prepared to not truly understand what is happening until the second round… They understood the rules, but it is difficult to grok why you should do anything" (Phil Schaefer)

The most-thumbed post links [arog's full rolling-teach script](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1pom0xhjmXX3j86xOwlDjawcojK7UNfb1AKGvth_tGlI/edit) (from the Wehrlegig Discord). Its structure is directly relevant to this repo's format:

- A short pre-play overview: what the EIC is, both end conditions, VP and the Power award, negotiation boundaries ("Everything is negotiable, except family members, office holders…"), promise cards, the two career paths
- Then a phase-by-phase walkthrough of round 1 in board order, with bracketed stage directions at each decision point: `[give example of £5 vs £2 trade with promise card]`, `[Have player handle director of trade]`, `[Wait for an order to be opened - explain checks]`, `[Just do this and explain as you do it]`
- Deliberate deferrals inline: "London Season — Skip in round 1, will explain later," "Skip firms this game," "Governor — later." The dice-check system is taught only when the first order opens

That is a beat sheet with do-brackets and explicit deferral markers, independently invented. The rolling teach is not an alternative to a script; it's a script whose spine is the board.

"Rolling teach" is now standard vocabulary for heavy games: Heavy Cardboard publishes [rolling teach and play-through videos](https://boardgamegeek.com/video/394109/john-company-second-edition/john-company-2nd-edition-4p-rolling-teach-play-thr). Cole Wehrle has also run a full front-loaded teach ([SDHistCon 2021](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0mFNEdfCSZo)), so both modes exist even from the designer. No written Wehrle teaching-pedagogy commentary surfaced in the interviews checked.

## Teach vs scripted round: the actual comparison

The case for teach-up-front, from the [Food Chain Magnate thread](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2129341/teaching-the-game-first-vs-playing-to-learn) (Walter Deep's objections to playing-to-learn):

- "It's messy, since I can't explain systems in the ideal order." The game's trigger order is not the pedagogical order
- Some rules "come up very late in the game or not at all," so teach-by-trigger never covers them
- In heavy competitive games "opening moves can be key and mistakes brutally punishing," and an under-informed player is exploitable

The counter-evidence sits in the same thread: his group jumped into a learning game of FCM anyway, refused the offered reset, and "came away from the game with a pretty positive attitude."

The resolution the sources point to: what gates the rolling teach is the game's structure, not its weight. It works when the play order is a teachable order (John Company's ribbon), when round 1 legitimately skips subsystems, and when early mistakes are recoverable or the table agrees it's a learning game with takebacks. It fails when systems interlock so the ideal explanation order diverges from trigger order, and when opening blunders are punished. Both modes still need the same pre-play overview: goal, end conditions, and the one mechanism everything builds toward.

## Tutorial campaigns: Gloomhaven, Jaws of the Lion

The 31-page Learn to Play guide replaces the glossary rulebook for the first five scenarios, with simplified starting decks and maps printed directly in the spiral scenario book. "A masterclass in producing this sort of pamphlet" ([FauxHammer review](https://www.fauxhammer.com/news/gloomhaven-jaws-of-the-lion-review/)). The unlock sequence ([Tabletop Bellhop actual plays](https://tabletopbellhop.com/actual-plays/gloomhaven-jotl-scenario-4/), [Age of Miniatures](https://ageofminiatures.com/jaws-of-the-lion-review/)):

1. Roadside Ambush: scripted setup, tutorial-only reduced ability cards, move, attack, initiative
1. A Hole in the Wall: money, treasure, traps, doors, looting, area effects
1. The Black Ship: difficult terrain, experience, long rest, remaining conditions, perks, city events (reviewers note the curve jumps here)
1. A Ritual in Stone: elements, battle goals, full hands and real deck-building
1. Boss rules, then graduation to the full rulebook

Isaac Childres built it as a retail on-ramp to the Gloomhaven system because "the standard process of learning a text laden rulebook wasn't going to cut it" ([Way Too Many Games](https://waytoomany.games/2020/11/20/jaws-of-the-lion-makes-gloomhaven-newcomer-friendly/), [Tabletop Gaming interview](https://www.tabletopgaming.co.uk/features/jaws-of-victory-talking-jaws-of-the-lion-with-isaac-childres/)).

The known weakness: the tutorial is welded to campaign progress. A player joining at scenario 6+ faces the full ruleset with no on-ramp. Community fixes are workarounds, not solutions: replay the intro scenarios as a throwaway side session, restart the campaign, or hand an experienced player a leveled class ([BGG thread](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2452668/best-way-to-add-a-player-after-campaign-has-alread), [fan hero summary aid](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2795296/new-players-hero-summary)). No official catch-up exists. The lesson for this repo: a teach artifact must serve the person who wasn't there, which unlock-based products structurally can't.

## The TTRPG pattern

TTRPGs embed the rolling teach in the format itself, which is what this project is trying to give board games.

- The D&D Starter Set's adventure carries the pedagogy: pregens whose personal goals tie into the module, and an opening ambush that hits combat, exploration, and social play in a fixed teachable order. The module teaches the DM as much as the players ([Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/dnd/lost-mine-of-phandelver), [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/board-games/lost-mine-of-phandelver-is-still-a-great-way-to-start-a-dungeons-and-dragons-5e-campaign/), [Sly Flourish's DM guide](https://slyflourish.com/running_phandelver.html))
- Powered by the Apocalypse prints its pedagogy as GM agenda: "play to find out what happens" removes the you-played-it-wrong failure mode, and playbooks are self-teaching character sheets where the moves are the only rules the player needs ([Necropraxis on agendas](https://necropraxis.com/2018/08/13/apocalypse-world-agendas/), [Gnome Stew](https://gnomestew.com/the-gms-agenda-and-principles/))
- Convention one-shot culture: pregens because a slot can't absorb character creation, and "teach concentrically": "you don't need to talk about the initiative system until a fight breaks out" ([Roleplaying Tips](https://www.roleplayingtips.com/running-games/how-i-run-one-shots-great-for-convention-games-too/), [Katrina Ostrander on pregens](https://katrinaostrander.com/running-your-best-convention-one-shot-part-1-pre-gens/))
- The Alexandrian's open table is an onboarding institution: episodic structure that tolerates drop-in players, exactly what JotL's campaign can't ([Open Table Manifesto](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/38643/roleplaying-games/open-table-manifesto), [Gamemastery 101](https://thealexandrian.net/gamemastery-101))

Why it works there and not here, structurally: the GM is a living rules engine, so players only state intent and rules load reveals lazily on trigger. And with no hidden win condition and no competition, an under-informed player can't be exploited, which is the exact reason heavy competitive games resist rolling teaches. What board games can borrow: the rolling teach is the GM-as-rules-engine pattern (one player runs the round and narrates the legal options), the tutorial campaign is the module-as-teacher pattern, and taking the Chairman seat yourself is the GM modeling a turn.

## Other creative methods, attested

- The learning-game norm: non-scoring practice rounds "so players gain confidence making decisions without long-term consequences" ([Meeple Mountain](https://www.meeplemountain.com/articles/the-goal-of-the-game-is-to-win-how-to-teach-games-in-an-engaging-way-that-sticks/)); declared takebacks in a learning game; Pax Pamir's fast sandbox first game where "everyone just tries out the actions without agonising too much about the game state" ([tekeli.li thread](https://discussion.tekeli.li/t/teaching-pax-pamir-2nd/2575))
- Deferral with consent: "I'm going to skip past that and I will explain what each does as they come up, is that cool?" ([Tabletop Bellhop](https://tabletopbellhop.com/gaming-advice/teaching-boardgames/))
- Designer-provided cut-down starts: Food Chain Magnate's official intro game (reduced map and decks); JotL scenario 1's fully scripted setup. No established community name surfaced for "start from a mid-game snapshot" as a general practice, which reads as unexplored design space
- Pre-load the homework: send the rulebook or a video ahead, then play. The Pax Pamir remote teach in a 90-minute slot succeeded "because all 3 players had either read the manual or watched the video" (tekeli.li thread; note this corrects an earlier citation in our design session, since the 90-minute figure lives on the fan forum, not in SU&SD's own writing)
- Reference sheet per player, with the [Esoteric Order of Gamers summaries](https://www.orderofgamers.com/games/) as the standing fan library

Not found despite searching, so treat as folklore until sourced: "play the first round wrong on purpose" and "open hands first round" as named, documented techniques.
