# 1 · Pedagogy principles

The load-bearing research behind every format decision in this repo. Each principle names its source; the synthesis at the end is ours.

## Cognitive load theory, applied to rules

Working memory holds a handful of new items. A rulebook read aloud front to back overflows it in minutes, which is why the strongest consensus advice is structural rather than stylistic.

- Chunk the game and announce the chunks. A professional educator on BGG frames teaching as managing intrinsic load: break the game into parts (combat, movement, market), tackle them one at a time, and state up front which sections you'll cover. [BGG: Teaching Games, a Professional Educator's Perspective](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3430117/teaching-games-a-professional-educators-perspectiv)
- The redundancy effect: making learners read dense text while listening to the same content adds load rather than removing it. The fix for a game with legible components is "read your two cards, ask me about any icon," never narration. [Structural Learning on redundancy](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/the-redundancy-effect), [Exhaustion Austin on cognitive load and rulebooks](https://exhaustionaustin.blog/2023/03/21/cognitive-load-theory-and-board-game-rules/)
- The expertise reversal effect: techniques that help novices (worked steps, integrated explanations) become unnecessary or harmful as learners gain knowledge. A teach sheet for the fifth play should look different from the first, which is why cue lists exist.

## Progressive disclosure

A UX term with a direct mapping: show only what the current decision needs, reveal the rest at the moment it becomes live. [UXPin on progressive disclosure](https://www.uxpin.com/studio/blog/what-is-progressive-disclosure/)

- Teach backwards from the win condition. A game store owner's framing: start with victory conditions and work backwards so players understand how to get there. [Forbes Advisor](https://www.forbes.com/advisor/t/how-to-teach-board-games/)
- Defer exceptions: "I'll explain that when it comes up." Front-loading every conditional drowns the table. [Hobbystack](https://hobbystack.net/guides/board-games-beginners-guide)
- Reveal at the moment of relevance, concretely: Wavelength's left/right bet is taught right before the first reveal, not during setup. Decrypto's interception is introduced at the top of round two, pointing at the note sheets, after players have already given three easy clues. Scoring lands better as a reveal than a preamble.
- State the goal immediately but defer the mechanism. Our style specimen's structural fix: name the win/lose condition in beat one, and still hold interception back until it goes live.

## The coached first turn

Set up the board and walk through the first turn with cards face up, because players learn faster when making real choices. [Forbes Advisor](https://www.forbes.com/advisor/t/how-to-teach-board-games/)

This is the bridge between lecture and play, and most of this repo's beats are built around it: Pax Pamir's market is taught by everyone taking a real turn, Risk: Europe runs a dry round of naming orders, Looot has everyone place one Viking before buildings exist.

## Strategy and agency

The standard advice says never teach strategy: it biases the game and steals the fun of working it out. [Hobbystack](https://hobbystack.net/guides/board-games-beginners-guide) The same sources concede the balance point, though: the job is making players aware of what can happen and what their options are, without telling them what they should do. [My Kind of Meeple](https://mykindofmeeple.com/teaching-board-games-to-new-players/), [Don't Eat the Meeples](https://donteatthemeeples.substack.com/p/nine-tips-for-teaching-board-games)

Our position splits the rule in two:

- Never prescribe moves ("open with X")
- Always expose the payoff structure, because players can't make independent decisions if they can't see it. "Looot scores (base + bonuses) × quantity, so collecting a bit of everything loses" is orientation, not coaching

Where a game's opening is genuinely opaque (Pax Pamir), a strategy section is allowed as a labeled exception on the sheet itself. Payoff-structure guidance that builds agency is exactly what most rulebooks and tutorials lack, and rectifying that is a goal of this project.

## Watch the table, not the notes

Leaning back, glazed eyes, or phones appearing means stop talking and start playing. [Hobbystack](https://hobbystack.net/guides/board-games-beginners-guide) This is also the strongest argument against reading a verbatim script: reading costs eye contact, and eye contact is the diagnostic.

## Why good teaches are rare

Rulebooks are arbitration documents optimized for mid-game lookup, and publishers ship one artifact to do two incompatible jobs. A teach script is teacher-facing: an audience of one per table and no commercial reason to exist. It requires pedagogical decisions on top of rules mastery, and iterating on it means repeatedly finding fresh naive players. The incentive is backwards too: an enthusiastic owner wants to convey the whole design, and completeness feels like respect.
